Public-outage replays

Would your design have survived it? Famous outages, turned from their published timelines into what your system would have felt, replayed against your model as built and with each strategy.

Slack, 4 January 2021, replayed against HotROD

  1. The outage and your result

    Pick one from Replay an outage…. The headline is your system as built: here 280 minutes out of SLO and 115,047 failed requests.

  2. What happened

    The published story in one paragraph, then its timeline, minute by minute.

  3. How it maps onto your system

    Your normal load, which of your components play each role, every assumption the replay makes, and the source. Slack gave no traffic number, so the 1.55× morning surge is an assumption to tune, and the replay says so.

  4. On the chart

    The timeline's events are marked on the chart, and every strategy is replayed through the same outage. Here none of HotROD's strategies shortens the 280 minutes out of SLO, though two customer replicas cut the failed requests by 70,582.

The outages

slack-2021-01-04The Monday after the holidays: a cold-cache surge, saturated gateways, packet loss, and autoscaling that couldn't provision.1.55× your peak, 1.5% of calls failing and +40 ms everywhere, autoscaling blocked for 74 minutes and then 4× slower
aws-use1-2025-10-20DynamoDB's DNS record emptied; EC2 launches were impaired for hours afterwards.managed-kv unreachable for 2 h 37 min, compute unable to launch for hours, then edge errors while health checks flap

Roles (edge, primary-db, managed-kv, compute, all) are resolved to your components automatically, or set them yourself:

hindcast outage model.json list
hindcast outage model.json slack-2021-01-04 autoscale.yaml --role primary-db=orders-db

Every replay cites its source, the company's own postmortem, and lists the assumptions it makes.
