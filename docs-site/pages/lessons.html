A level

  1. Pick a lesson

    Eighteen lessons, fifty-four levels, in four tracks. The picker shows your progress: the level you reached, or the stars you earned.

  2. The situation

    Each level tells a short story: launch day, the front page of Hacker News, a database brownout, the bill arriving.

  3. The objective

    "Serve 120 req/s with <1% errors and p99 < 300ms for 5s." The engine checks it while you play.

  4. Hold it

    The bar fills while the objective holds; break it and it resets. Some levels have a window: the system must ride out what happens in it, not recover afterwards.

  5. Change the design

    Add components from the palette, scale what you have, connect things differently.

  6. Play

    Space plays and pauses. 1×, 2×, 4× speed.

When you're stuck, and when you win

  • Hint: after a few seconds out of SLO the level asks a question that points at the evidence (a trace, a red card). [ I'm stuck: show the answer ] under it explains what is going on and what to try.
  • Show me: next to the answer, it puts the level's reference design on the board (undo takes it back). Press ▶ or Replay level and watch it hold.
  • Replay level: replays the level with the design on the board; your work is never thrown away.
  • Why it worked: every passed level ends with what it taught and the numbers the system held.
  • The finish card recaps every level, gives up to three stars for the bill, and leads to the next lesson.

Every level is played by a test (test/lessons.test.ts): the design the previous level left must fail it, the reference design must pass it, both with the traffic jitter the app adds.
