The chart and the strategies

Everything shares one time axis, stacked like a trading terminal. The screenshot shows HotROD, one of the three built-in reference systems: 40 minutes of real traffic with a push past capacity, and four strategies replayed over it.

  1. Views

    Every part of Bottleneck is one click away: the backtest, the Live board, Lessons, Sandbox, Compare and Studio.

  2. Range and bucket size

    ALL, 24H, 6H, 1H, 15M. ⌘/ctrl + wheel zooms around the cursor. The bucket size is the data's own resolution: here 30 s.

  3. p99 latency

    Measured p99 as candles: open, high, low and close of each group of buckets. The dashed grey line is the model replaying the system as built. Each strategy has its own coloured line with a shaded 10–90% band. The dashed red line is the SLO.

  4. Incidents

    Shaded columns are stretches out of SLO. Here, the push to twice the normal load: the as-built system timed out for minutes. Click any moment to open it on the board as a replay.

  5. Errors

    Measured error rate and each strategy's replay. "Clients retry twice, no budget" turns the overload into 100% errors: the retries pile on top of a saturated database lock.

  6. Cost per hour

    From the bill (measured), as built, and per strategy: replica-hours at the billed price, usage-billed requests, data transfer across zones and regions.

  7. Savings

    Cumulative dollars saved or spent against as built: the equity curve. A line above zero saves money.

  8. Strategies, ranked

    As built and each strategy with $/month and minutes out of SLO. Strategies that keep the SLO rank first, then the cheapest. Click to focus one, use the eye to hide it, + strategy to write a new one.

  9. Worst moment, and why

    For the focused strategy: when it was worst and which component limited it, with its load against capacity and its queue. "mysql at 56% of capacity (11.2 req/s on 2 × 9.9 req/s), 85 ms wait". open on board replays that moment.

  10. Model accuracy

    How well this model predicted recorded runs it never saw, check by check, against the targets: p99 within 20%, breaking point within 15%, cost within 10%. Shown with every result, so you know how far to trust it.

  11. The verdict

    One sentence for the focused strategy: what it saves or costs per month and what it would have done to users. It is the same sentence the pull-request check posts.

  12. The numbers

    Net $/month, minutes out of SLO, failed requests, error budget used, peak p99 and replica-hours. Each comes with its 10–90% range from 30 paired draws of the model's uncertain capacities.

  13. System

    Switch between the reference systems, or your own workspace once you've opened files.

  14. Card

    A Studio card of the focused strategy at post size: the chart drawing itself, then the verdict, with the model's measured accuracy.

  15. Replay an outage

    Cloudflare 2019, GitHub 2018, Slack 2021, AWS us-east-1 2025, replayed against this system as built and with each strategy. See public-outage replays.

  16. Open files

    Your own data: an import manifest with its metric, trace and bill files, or a dataset.json and model.json from the CLI, plus strategy files. You can also drop them anywhere on the screen. See bring your data.

Incidents

Every stretch the focused strategy spent out of SLO, with its cause.

  1. The tester tabs

    Overview, Incidents, Endpoints, Changes and Model all describe the focused strategy. The count on Incidents is how many stretches it spent out of SLO.

  2. One row per incident

    When it started, how long it lasted, peak p99, peak errors, and why: the busiest component and how close it was to its limit. When nothing was near its limit, the row says so. HotROD's early incidents are its driver's load-dependent errors, not capacity. board ↗ opens that moment as a replay.

Endpoints

Each endpoint is judged by its own SLO and priced by its own path through the system. This is the Astronomy Shop with an autoscaled frontend.

  1. One row per endpoint

    Endpoint ids come from the traces' route templates, so /api/products/{productId} is one endpoint, not thousands.

  2. Its own SLO

    Set per endpoint in the import manifest (model.endpoints.<id>.slo). A checkout that must answer in 800 ms is held to that, not to the system average.

  3. Failed requests

    As built → with the strategy, over the whole history.

  4. Minutes out of its SLO

    Green when the strategy removes time out of SLO, red when it adds it.

  5. $ per million requests

    Compute along the endpoint's critical path, usage-billed requests and data transfer. Here a checkout costs about 17 times a product page ($5.15 vs $0.31 per million) because of the services it calls.

Changes

  1. The strategy, as YAML

    Edit it here and the backtest reruns as you type. The same file works with the CLI and the pull-request check. Every option is listed under strategies.

  2. What changed, and what is assumed

    A plain-English list of every change and every assumption it rests on. For example, a Graviton move without your own benchmark says it assumes equal throughput per vCPU.

Any moment, on the board

Click any moment on the chart, or open on board next to the worst moment. The system opens as a live canvas replaying that stretch of traffic through the model, ten times faster.

  1. The model as a board

    Every component with its fitted capacity, price and replicas, and requests flowing along the call paths from the traces. In-process dependencies (HotROD's simulated MySQL and Redis) sit next to the services that host them. The board is a simplified drawing; the backtest numbers come from the model itself.

  2. Backtest these edits

    Change anything on the board — add a replica, put a cache in front, resize — and press this. The edits come back to the chart as a new strategy, replayed over the whole history.

  3. Back to the chart

    Returns to the backtest without changes.
